## Data Insights

### Disease Impact by Country

#### Chile

**Surveillance Programme**

Caligidosis is monitored through the Specific Health Programme for the Surveillance and Control of Caligidosis (PSEVC Caligidosis, Resolución Exenta N°60/2022) run by the National Fisheries and Aquaculture Service (Sernapesca). It covers high-surveillance centres (CAV) farming Atlantic salmon and rainbow trout in the Los Lagos, Aysén and Magallanes regions. The programme sets a threshold of 3.0 average ovigerous females (OF) per fish, and a CAV is classified as a high dissemination centre (CAD) when its first monitoring after the official treatment window of its farm grouping shows loads at or above that threshold.[1](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/03/Informe-Situacion-Sanitaria-Salmonicultura-Ano-2025.pdf)

**Parasite Loads in 2025**

In Los Lagos, the highest weekly average load was 3.0 OF (weeks 01 and 18) and the lowest was 1.6 OF (weeks 45 and 46). In Aysén, the highest was 3.6 OF (week 42, October) and the lowest was 1.7 OF. Magallanes had considerably lower loads, with a maximum weekly average of 0.3 OF. Compared with 2024, loads showed a slight downward trend in Los Lagos and a slight upward trend in Aysén, while water temperature remained stable in the three regions.[1](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/03/Informe-Situacion-Sanitaria-Salmonicultura-Ano-2025.pdf)

**Centres Above the Threshold**

In Los Lagos and Aysén, the weekly proportion of CAV with an average load of 3.0 OF or more ranged from 8.4% (week 49, December) to 25.4% (week 01, January), while in Magallanes the maximum was 2.6% (week 39, September). Over 2025, 211 CAV recorded loads of 3.0 OF or more, mostly in the heaviest production stage (T3), which accounted for 43.6% (92 centres). Sernapesca defines the production stages by fish weight: T1 is up to 1.5 kg for Atlantic salmon (up to 1.0 kg for rainbow trout and coho salmon), T2 is above 1.5 and up to 3.0 kg (above 1.0 and up to 2.0 kg), and T3 is above 3.0 kg (above 2.0 kg).[1](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/03/Informe-Situacion-Sanitaria-Salmonicultura-Ano-2025.pdf)

**Antiparasitic Use in 2024**

Sernapesca reports antiparasitic use for production cycles closed in 2024, meaning those whose harvest finished between 1 January and 31 December. The sample comprises 301 cycles: 181 of Atlantic salmon, 97 of coho salmon and 23 of rainbow trout. Hydrogen peroxide is reported separately from the other antiparasitics because of its much larger volumes and its lower environmental risk, as it breaks down quickly into water and oxygen.[2](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2025/07/Informe-Uso-Antimicrobianos-y-Antiparasitarios-Ano-2024.pdf)

Antiparasitics other than hydrogen peroxide (azamethiphos, emamectin benzoate, deltamethrin and hexaflumuron) totalled 4.77 t, giving a national consumption index of 4.29 g per tonne produced. Azamethiphos and hexaflumuron accounted for 97.02% of this use, with emamectin benzoate and deltamethrin used to a lesser extent. All are given as immersion baths except emamectin benzoate, which is delivered in the feed.[2](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2025/07/Informe-Uso-Antimicrobianos-y-Antiparasitarios-Ano-2024.pdf)

Hydrogen peroxide, which Sernapesca describes as the product applied to control caligidosis, was used as an immersion bath, almost entirely in Atlantic salmon. A total of 12,112 t were applied, giving an index of 10,890 g per tonne produced. In 2024 the antiparasitic consumption index decreased while the hydrogen peroxide index increased significantly, which Sernapesca interprets as a preference of companies for this active ingredient because of its comparative advantages.[2](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2025/07/Informe-Uso-Antimicrobianos-y-Antiparasitarios-Ano-2024.pdf)
