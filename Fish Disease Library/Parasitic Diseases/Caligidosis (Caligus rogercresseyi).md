## Data Insights

### Disease Impact by Country

#### Chile

**Surveillance Programme**

Caligidosis is monitored through the Specific Health Programme for the Surveillance and Control of Caligidosis (PSEVC Caligidosis, Resolución Exenta N°60/2022) run by the National Fisheries and Aquaculture Service (Sernapesca). It covers high-surveillance centres (CAV) farming Atlantic salmon and rainbow trout in the Los Lagos, Aysén and Magallanes regions. The programme sets a threshold of 3.0 average ovigerous females (OF) per fish, and a CAV is classified as a high dissemination centre (CAD) when its first monitoring after the official treatment window of its farm grouping shows loads at or above that threshold.[1](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/03/Informe-Situacion-Sanitaria-Salmonicultura-Ano-2025.pdf)

**Parasite Loads in 2025**

In Los Lagos, the highest weekly average load was 3.0 OF (weeks 01 and 18) and the lowest was 1.6 OF (weeks 45 and 46). In Aysén, the highest was 3.6 OF (week 42, October) and the lowest was 1.7 OF. Magallanes had considerably lower loads, with a maximum weekly average of 0.3 OF. Compared with 2024, loads showed a slight downward trend in Los Lagos and a slight upward trend in Aysén, while water temperature remained stable in the three regions.[1](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/03/Informe-Situacion-Sanitaria-Salmonicultura-Ano-2025.pdf)

**Centres Above the Threshold**

In Los Lagos and Aysén, the weekly proportion of CAV with an average load of 3.0 OF or more ranged from 8.4% (week 49, December) to 25.4% (week 01, January), while in Magallanes the maximum was 2.6% (week 39, September). Over 2025, 211 CAV recorded loads of 3.0 OF or more, mostly in the heaviest production stage (T3), which accounted for 43.6% (92 centres). Sernapesca defines the production stages by fish weight: T1 is up to 1.5 kg for Atlantic salmon (up to 1.0 kg for rainbow trout and coho salmon), T2 is above 1.5 and up to 3.0 kg (above 1.0 and up to 2.0 kg), and T3 is above 3.0 kg (above 2.0 kg).[1](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/03/Informe-Situacion-Sanitaria-Salmonicultura-Ano-2025.pdf)

**Antiparasitic Use in 2025**

Sernapesca reports antiparasitic use for production cycles closed in 2025, meaning those whose harvest finished between 1 January and 31 December. The sample comprises 315 cycles: 195 of Atlantic salmon, 107 of coho salmon and 13 of rainbow trout. These products are used mainly to control *Caligus rogercresseyi*, together with non-pharmacological immersion strategies, whose use increased by 36.1% compared with 2024 (from 495 to 674 uses). Hydrogen peroxide is reported separately from the other antiparasitics because of its much larger volumes and its lower environmental impact, as it breaks down quickly into water and oxygen.[2](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/03/Informe-de-uso-de-antimicrobianos-ano-2025.pdf)

Antiparasitics other than hydrogen peroxide (azamethiphos, emamectin benzoate, deltamethrin and hexaflumuron) totalled 3.03 t, giving a national consumption index of 2.52 g per tonne produced, 41.26% lower than in 2024. Azamethiphos and hexaflumuron accounted for 91.2% of this use, with emamectin benzoate and deltamethrin used to a lesser extent. All are given as immersion baths except emamectin benzoate, which is delivered in the feed. Use was concentrated in Atlantic salmon and, to a lesser extent, rainbow trout, and no use was recorded in coho salmon, which Sernapesca relates to its lower susceptibility to caligidosis.[2](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/03/Informe-de-uso-de-antimicrobianos-ano-2025.pdf)

Hydrogen peroxide, which Sernapesca describes as the product applied to control caligidosis, was used as an immersion bath, mainly in Atlantic salmon in Los Lagos and Aysén. A total of 12,947 t were applied, giving an index of 10,795 g per tonne produced, which did not change significantly compared with 2024 (0.87%). Sernapesca considers it an effective alternative with a lower environmental impact for the control of caligidosis.[2](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/03/Informe-de-uso-de-antimicrobianos-ano-2025.pdf)

**Regulatory Framework**

Caligidosis is classified in Chile as a high-risk disease of List 2 for fish, caused by infestation with the ectoparasite *Caligus rogercresseyi*.[3](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/chi112982.pdf) Its control is regulated through a specific health programme established under the General Law of Fisheries and Aquaculture and the Regulation on Protection, Control and Eradication of High-Risk Diseases (DS N°319 of 2001).[3](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/chi112982.pdf)[4](https://www.subpesca.cl/portal/617/articles-103570_recurso_2.pdf) The programme was first approved in 2009 (Resolución Exenta N°2.117), replaced in 2012 (Resolución Exenta N°1.141), approved again in 2015 (Resolución Exenta N°13) and modified in 2019 (Resolución Exenta N°594).[3](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/chi112982.pdf)[4](https://www.subpesca.cl/portal/617/articles-103570_recurso_2.pdf) The 2025 Sernapesca health report refers to Resolución Exenta N°60/2022 as the framework of the programme.[1](https://www.sernapesca.cl/app/uploads/2026/03/Informe-Situacion-Sanitaria-Salmonicultura-Ano-2025.pdf)

Its stated aims are the early detection of the parasite and its loads and the control of its spread, through coordinated treatments, rotation of active ingredients, control of dissemination and early harvest of infested biomass.[4](https://www.subpesca.cl/portal/617/articles-103570_recurso_2.pdf) Since the 2012 version, the programme has defined a high dissemination centre and treats a radius of 5 nautical miles around it as an infected zone.[3](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/chi112982.pdf) The 2019 modification allows Sernapesca to set exceptional conditions for new treatment alternatives, after assessing the evidence provided by the applicant.[4](https://www.subpesca.cl/portal/617/articles-103570_recurso_2.pdf)

The regulation also allows the declaration of a sanitary emergency for List 2 diseases. In November 2011, Sernapesca declared one for caligidosis in salmonid farm grouping N°10, because parasite loads exceeded the high infestation level for adults and juveniles even after three consecutive coordinated treatments.[5](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/chi107336.pdf) For six months, farms in the grouping had to report average parasite loads weekly, and coordinated treatment of micro-zones was activated when a centre reached an average of 6 or more lice. Centres with 9 or more weekly events above 6 lice between weeks 26 and 44 that kept such loads after treatment had to start harvesting within 3 days of notification.[5](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/chi107336.pdf)

**Antiparasitic Use and Efficacy in 2018**

The technical report cited in Resolución Exenta N°594 states that five active ingredients were registered against *C. rogercresseyi*: azamethiphos, cypermethrin and deltamethrin, used as immersion baths in sea farms, and lufenuron and emamectin benzoate, used in freshwater centres or at stocking in the sea.[4](https://www.subpesca.cl/portal/617/articles-103570_recurso_2.pdf) In 2018, 1,396 antiparasitic immersion treatments were reported, 77% of them with azamethiphos, while hydrogen peroxide, then used under a special use authorisation, accounted for less than 0.05% of treatments.[4](https://www.subpesca.cl/portal/617/articles-103570_recurso_2.pdf) Efficacy was highest for azamethiphos, followed by deltamethrin, but the report notes a decline in average efficacy, which had fluctuated between 90 and 100% in 2016-2017, with some areas reporting 80% or less.[4](https://www.subpesca.cl/portal/617/articles-103570_recurso_2.pdf)
