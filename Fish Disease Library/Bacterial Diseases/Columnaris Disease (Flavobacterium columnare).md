## Overview

### What is Columnaris Disease (Flavobacterium columnare)?

Columnaris disease is caused by *Flavobacterium columnare*, a Gram-negative, non-flagellated bacterium (family Flavobacteriaceae, phylum Bacteroidota) that is either non-motile or moves by gliding rather than via flagella [1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jare.2014.10.009). It is one of the three species recognized as the "big three" fish-pathogenic flavobacteria, alongside *F. psychrophilum* (bacterial cold-water disease) and *F. branchiophilum* (bacterial gill disease), each covered in their own dedicated chapter [1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jare.2014.10.009).

*F. columnare* has a far broader host range than its sibling species, affecting both wild and cultured warm-water freshwater fish — including carp, channel catfish, goldfish, eel, perch, salmonids, and tilapia — and is distributed worldwide; in the US channel catfish industry it is the second most prevalent disease-causing bacterium after *Edwardsiella ictaluri*, with yearly losses estimated at US$30 million [2](https://doi.org/10.1186/1297-9716-44-27).

## Clinical Signs of Columnaris Disease (Flavobacterium columnare)

### Common Signs

- **Physical Signs:**
    - Pale necrotic gill lesions in young fish; in chronic cases, pale skin discoloration starting at the base of the dorsal fin that extends laterally to encircle the fish ("saddleback disease"), erosion progressing toward the caudal peduncle ("peduncle disease"), and ulceration of the oral mucosa ("mouthrot," or "cotton wool disease" in tropical fish) [2](https://doi.org/10.1186/1297-9716-44-27).

- **Behavioral Changes:**
    - **Reduced feeding:** Painful oral lesions render affected fish anorectic, which can itself be a cause of death independent of the systemic infection [2](https://doi.org/10.1186/1297-9716-44-27).

**Progression of Clinical Signs**

- **Advanced Stages:** Oral lesions can spread to the mandible and maxilla, and secondary fungal or bacterial infection of skin ulcers is common [2](https://doi.org/10.1186/1297-9716-44-27).

### Causes of Columnaris Disease (Flavobacterium columnare)

**Etiology**

- **Key Virulence Factors:** Virulence is morphotype-dependent: of three colony morphotypes studied, only the Rhizoid morphotype was virulent in rainbow trout, associated with a distinct internally organized cell structure, a secreted 13 kDa protein, and outer membrane vesicles [3](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2180-14-170).

**Risk Factors**

- **Environmental Factors:** Transmission and severity increase with rising temperature and organic matter load, with the gills serving as the major release site for the bacterium [2](https://doi.org/10.1186/1297-9716-44-27).

**Preventive Measures**

## Conclusion

Columnaris disease stands apart from its sibling flavobacterial diseases in host range and ecology: where *F. psychrophilum* and *F. branchiophilum* are pathogens of cold-water salmonid hatcheries, *F. columnare* affects a far broader range of warm-water freshwater fish worldwide and is a major economic burden in the US channel catfish industry.
