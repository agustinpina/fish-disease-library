## Overview

### What is Bacterial Gill Disease (Flavobacterium branchiophilum)?

Bacterial Gill Disease (BGD) is caused by *Flavobacterium branchiophilum*, a Gram-negative, non-flagellated bacterium (family Flavobacteriaceae, phylum Bacteroidota) that is either non-motile or moves by gliding rather than via flagella [1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jare.2014.10.009). It is one of the three species recognized as the "big three" fish-pathogenic flavobacteria, alongside *F. psychrophilum* (bacterial cold-water disease) and *F. columnare* (columnaris disease), each covered in their own dedicated chapter [1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jare.2014.10.009).

*F. branchiophilum* causes BGD with a tropism restricted to the gill epithelium, not normally invading deeper tissue, but severe infections can still produce morbidity and mortality of 10–75% [2](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfd.14119).

## Data Insights

### Disease Impact by Country

#### Slovenia

- **Incidence:**
    - A survey of Slovenian farmed rainbow trout confirmed the presence of *F. branchiophilum* (8 isolates characterized) — the first study to characterize AMR in this species using the broth microdilution method [2](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfd.14119).

- **Treatment & Management:**
    - Isolates could not be reliably classified as wild-type or non-wild-type with current methods, reflecting a broader diagnostic and surveillance gap for this species [2](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfd.14119).

## Conclusion

*F. branchiophilum* remains a persistent but far less-studied threat to gill health in freshwater salmonid aquaculture than its sibling species *F. psychrophilum*. It is the most fastidious of the three major fish-pathogenic flavobacteria, and this has limited even basic antimicrobial susceptibility characterization — a 2025 Slovenian study could not reliably classify its isolates as wild-type or non-wild-type with current testing methods.
