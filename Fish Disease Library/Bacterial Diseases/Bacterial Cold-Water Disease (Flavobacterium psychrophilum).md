## Overview

### What is Bacterial Cold-Water Disease (*Flavobacterium psychrophilum*)?

Bacterial Cold-Water Disease (BCWD, the term generally used in North America) — also known as Rainbow Trout Fry Syndrome (RTFS) in Europe — is caused by *Flavobacterium psychrophilum*, a Gram-negative, non-flagellated bacterium (family Flavobacteriaceae, phylum Bacteroidota) that is either non-motile or moves by gliding rather than via flagella [1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jare.2014.10.009). It is one of the three species recognized as the "big three" fish-pathogenic flavobacteria, alongside *F. columnare* (columnaris disease) and *F. branchiophilum* (bacterial gill disease) [1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jare.2014.10.009).

*F. psychrophilum* is a psychrophilic pathogen of cold-water salmonid hatcheries — principally rainbow trout (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), Atlantic salmon (*Salmo salar*), and coho salmon (*O. kisutch*) — causing skin/gill lesions and systemic disease with reported mortality of 30–90% in juvenile fish, and is reported from Europe, North America, Chile, Japan, Korea, and Australia [4](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfd.14119) [13](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13567-017-0448-z). Its host range is wider than salmonids: in China it has been isolated in recent years from farmed cyprinids — crucian carp, grass carp, and silver carp — and identified as a causal agent of overwintering mortality syndrome [16](https://doi.org/10.3390/ani16101465) [17](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2026.1751566).

## Clinical Signs of Bacterial Cold-Water Disease (*Flavobacterium psychrophilum*)

### Common Signs

- **Physical Signs:**
    - Disease episodes typically occur at 10–14°C, with necrotic lesions on the skin surrounding the dorsal fin and tail. Affected fish show haemorrhage (mouth, dorsal fin, operculum, pelvic fin, trunk), splenomegaly with blurred spleen margins, trunk lesions, and an eroded tail and mouth [13](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13567-017-0448-z).

**Progression of Clinical Signs**

- **Early Stages:** Disease onset is closely tied to water temperature. Experimentally raising water temperature accelerated the appearance of clinical disease, while increasing salinity delayed onset and improved survival, suggesting a window in early infection where environmental management can buy time for diagnosis and treatment [9](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfd.70037).
- **Advanced Stages:** Untreated RTFS/BCWD progresses to the full lesion picture described above with high mortality.
- **Impact on Fish Health:** Both live and dead fish shed *F. psychrophilum* into the water, but dead fish shed the bacterium at substantially higher rates and for longer durations than live fish, meaning that mortality events feed forward into further transmission pressure within a population if not managed promptly [10](https://doi.org/10.1128/spectrum.03601-23).

### Causes of Bacterial Cold-Water Disease (*Flavobacterium psychrophilum*)

**Etiology**

- **Causative Agent:** *F. psychrophilum* is a Gram-negative rod (members of the genus measure 0.3–0.5 μm in diameter) that is non-flagellated and either non-motile or displays gliding motility; it is fastidious and generally does not grow on standard media such as trypticase soy agar [1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jare.2014.10.009). It shows limited genomic diversity worldwide, with the MLST clonal complex CC-ST10 being the main clonal complex infecting farmed rainbow trout worldwide [5](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2018.00138).
- **Key Virulence Factors:** Colony spreading motility and proteolytic activity are required for pathogenicity — in a comparative study, the only non-virulent isolate tested was deficient in both traits [6](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2019.01711). The *gldD* and *gldG* genes, part of the gliding-motility/Type IX secretion system (T9SS), are required for adhesion, biofilm formation, proteolytic activity, and full virulence; mutants deficient in either gene showed significantly reduced virulence in rainbow trout challenge models [7](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2017.02168).
- **Transmission Methods:**
    - **Horizontal Transmission:** Both live and dead fish shed *F. psychrophilum* into the surrounding water; dead fish shed the bacterium at markedly higher rates and for longer durations than live fish, and shedding dynamics vary by bacterial genetic variant and host species [10](https://doi.org/10.1128/spectrum.03601-23).
    - **Vertical / Egg-Associated Transmission:** *F. psychrophilum* has been isolated from milt, ovarian fluids, and in close association with eggs, supporting a vertical transmission route that operates alongside horizontal transmission [11](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9050971).

**Risk Factors**

- **Environmental Factors:** In one experimental study, raising water temperature to 18 °C shortened the time to clinical disease — an unexpected result, since outbreaks are typically reported at colder temperatures — while 1% salinity delayed onset and improved cohabitant survival (42.6% vs 17.9%) [9](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfd.70037).
- **Farm Management Practices:** Prompt removal of dead and moribund fish is a key control point, since dead fish are disproportionate shedders of *F. psychrophilum* into hatchery and farm water [10](https://doi.org/10.1128/spectrum.03601-23).

### Diagnosis

**Diagnostic Methods**

- **Clinical Examination:** Gross lesions (haemorrhage and trunk lesions) provide a presumptive diagnosis, but clinical signs overlap with other bacterial diseases, so laboratory confirmation is required for species-level diagnosis.
- **Laboratory Tests:**
    - **Bacterial Culture:** *F. psychrophilum* is fastidious and requires specialized low-nutrient media (e.g., TYES or Shieh's medium); it does not grow on trypticase soy agar [1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jare.2014.10.009).
    - **PCR-Based Methods:** A quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) assay targeting the *rpoC* gene allows highly sensitive and specific detection and quantification of *F. psychrophilum* directly from water and fish tissue samples without the need for culturing [12](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2180-14-105). A multiplex PCR assay enables simultaneous, accurate differentiation of *F. psychrophilum* from two other major salmonid pathogens, *Yersinia ruckeri* and *Aeromonas salmonicida* subsp. *salmonicida*, in a single reaction [3](https://doi.org/10.4142/jvs.2010.11.3.235).
    - **Environmental DNA/RNA (Water Sampling):** In a 12-week study of two freshwater recirculating aquaculture system (RAS) units on a commercial Atlantic salmon pre-smolt farm, *F. psychrophilum* was detected in water samples at higher and more stable levels than in gill swabs, which showed only minor fluctuations. This supports non-lethal water sampling as a way to track the bacterium in RAS, although a positive water sample does not by itself indicate active infection — no clinical *F. psychrophilum* disease was observed during the study [18](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aquaculture.2025.743060).

**Differential Diagnosis**

- **Distinguishing BCWD/RTFS from Other Diseases:** A multiplex PCR assay developed for *F. psychrophilum*, *Y. ruckeri* and *A. salmonicida* subsp. *salmonicida* allows all three to be identified from the same sample [3](https://doi.org/10.4142/jvs.2010.11.3.235).

### Treatment and Prevention

**Treatment Options**

- **Current Treatments:**
    - **Antibiotics:** Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) profiling of *F. psychrophilum* shows a consistent pattern across geographically distant farming regions: florfenicol retains high effectiveness, while resistance to oxytetracycline and quinolones is widespread. In Slovenia, 86.3% of isolates were non-wild-type (reduced susceptibility) for oxytetracycline and enrofloxacin and 90.2% for oxolinic acid, while all isolates were wild-type for florfenicol and erythromycin; reduced oxolinic acid susceptibility was linked to a T83A or T83V substitution in the *gyrA* gene [4](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfd.14119). In Chile, MIC-based testing found non-wild-type frequencies of 70% for oxytetracycline and 38–45% for the quinolones oxolinic acid, flumequine, and enrofloxacin, but only 2% for florfenicol (8% by disk diffusion) [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2016.01880).

- **Vaccines:**
    - **Subunit and Attenuated Candidates:** Vaccine development has been hampered by the bacterium's antigenic and genetic diversity. Candidate antigens — OmpH, OmpA, FspA, and lipopolysaccharide — have been identified using sera from convalescent rainbow trout, and an attenuated *exbD2* mutant strain achieved 81.8% relative percent survival (RPS) after intramuscular immunization and challenge [8](https://doi.org/10.1111/1751-7915.12099).
    - **Polyvalent Immersion Vaccine:** A polyvalent immersion vaccine combining three formalin-inactivated *F. psychrophilum* strains gave 84% RPS in rainbow trout fry against a heterologous strain challenge, with vaccinated fish also showing a delayed mortality onset compared to unvaccinated controls [13](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13567-017-0448-z).
    - **Vaccine Implementation:** Commercial vaccines against *F. psychrophilum* remain available in only a limited number of countries (Chile, Norway), and none are suitable for the juvenile fry that bear the highest disease burden, leaving antibiotics as the primary recourse during an outbreak in most regions [13](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13567-017-0448-z).

- **Experimental Alternatives:**
    - **Phage-Derived Endolysins (in vitro only):** Ely174, an endolysin from an *F. psychrophilum* bacteriophage, lysed *F. psychrophilum* in vitro once the outer membrane had been permeabilised. Protein engineering improved its activity and thermal stability, and fusing it with the C-terminal domain of the Type IX secretion system protein SprA let it lyse untreated *F. psychrophilum* cells without an outer-membrane permeabiliser — although slowly and only at high concentrations [19](https://doi.org/10.1128/aem.01891-25).

**Preventive Measures**

- **Biosecurity Protocols:**
    - Iodine-based egg disinfection, standard in salmonid hatcheries, reduces surface bacterial load but does not sterilise eggs, and intra-ovum infection has been reported [11](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9050971).
    - Experimental: bacteriophages showed short-term (24 h) control of *F. psychrophilum* on eyed eggs without affecting egg survival [11](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9050971).
    - Prompt removal of dead and moribund fish to reduce the disproportionate shedding load they contribute to hatchery and farm water [10](https://doi.org/10.1128/spectrum.03601-23).

- **Farm Management Practices:**
    - Managing water temperature and salinity where feasible, which experimentally influenced RTFS onset and fry survival [9](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfd.70037).

- **Genetic Selection:**
    - Selective breeding for BCWD resistance is already applied in commercial rainbow trout lines. In one important breeding population, adding genomic information raised prediction accuracy for resistance from 0.27 (pedigree-based) to 0.36. The trait behaved as oligogenic there, with its largest association peaks on chromosomes Omy25 and Omy8 [14](https://doi.org/10.3389/fgene.2025.1582138).
    - Resistance architecture can differ between populations. In two French commercial rainbow trout populations, 29-day survival after bath challenge was 71.3% vs 50.7%, and the 11 QTLs detected showed limited overlap between the two populations. Complement-system genes (e.g., *C3*, *Cfb*) sat in two of the QTL regions. Breeding programmes may therefore need population-specific genetic markers [15](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-025-12231-3).

## Data Insights

### Disease Impact by Country

#### Slovenia

- **Incidence:**
    - A survey of Slovenian farmed rainbow trout confirmed the presence of *F. psychrophilum* (51 isolates characterized) [4](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfd.14119).

- **Treatment & Management:**
    - 86.3% of isolates showed reduced susceptibility to oxytetracycline and enrofloxacin, and 90.2% to oxolinic acid, while all isolates were wild-type for florfenicol and erythromycin [4](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfd.14119).

#### Chile

- **Incidence:**
    - *F. psychrophilum* is described as the most important bacterial pathogen of freshwater farmed salmonids in Chile, with 125 isolates characterized from reared salmonids showing clinical signs of disease [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2016.01880).

- **Treatment & Management:**
    - MIC-based epidemiological cut-off analysis found non-wild-type frequencies of 70% for oxytetracycline and 38–45% for the quinolones oxolinic acid, flumequine, and enrofloxacin, while only 2% of isolates (8% by disk diffusion) showed reduced susceptibility to florfenicol — a resistance pattern broadly consistent with the Slovenian data above despite the geographic distance between the two surveys [2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2016.01880).

## Conclusion

*F. psychrophilum* causes substantial, recurring losses in cold-water salmonid hatcheries worldwide. The most effective management strategies currently available combine water-temperature and salinity management, prompt removal of dead and moribund fish to limit shedding, iodine-based egg disinfection to reduce egg-associated bacterial load, and judicious antibiotic use guided by local resistance surveillance — florfenicol remains broadly effective across geographically distant farming regions (Slovenia, Chile), even where resistance to oxytetracycline and quinolones is already widespread. No commercially robust, broadly available vaccine yet exists, and none of the limited vaccines that do exist are suitable for the juvenile fry that bear the greatest disease burden, leaving antibiotics as the default response to most outbreaks. Genomic selection for BCWD resistance in rainbow trout offers a complementary, longer-term route, although resistance loci can differ between breeding populations.

Bacteriophage-based biocontrol, while still preliminary, merits continued investigation as a non-antibiotic intervention, particularly around the egg stage where vertical transmission can be intercepted. Country-level surveillance data remains sparse outside of the handful of farming regions covered here, and discrete, well-documented outbreak case studies — as opposed to general AMR surveys — were not identified for this chapter and remain an open item for a future research pass.
